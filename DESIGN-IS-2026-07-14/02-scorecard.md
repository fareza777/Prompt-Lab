# Dieter Rams scorecard

1. Good design is innovative — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: Builder, Optimizer, Compare, templates, and library form one coherent workflow (`01-evidence.md#structure-and-usefulness`).
   Justification: It improves a familiar prompt-tool pattern, but no evidence shows a restrained interaction unique across five peer products.

2. Good design makes a product useful — Score: 2/3
   Evidence: All six tasks are directly reachable, but Builder presents 41 controls and attachment is not keyboard reachable (`01-evidence.md#structure-and-usefulness`).
   Justification: The primary task works end-to-end, with avoidable control and accessibility friction.

3. Good design is aesthetic — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: The cyan/dark editorial system is recognizable, but two current portrait store frames contain missing rendered glyphs (`01-evidence.md#product-and-listing`).
   Justification: A consistent system is present, but corrupted release media is one jarring presentation failure.

4. Good design makes a product understandable — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Multiple primary form controls lack associated labels, mobile Search is unnamed, and heuristic terminology needs explanation (`01-evidence.md#accessibility-and-interaction-details`).
   Justification: More than three important controls or concepts require inference from context.

5. Good design is unobtrusive — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Builder exposes 24 chips before its action and fixed navigation/chrome occupies substantial mobile space (`01-evidence.md#structure-and-usefulness`).
   Justification: Selection chrome competes with the user's prompt and output.

6. Good design is honest — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: The current Play description is supportable, but the landing page still uses multiple inflations such as “Perfect Prompt”, “production-ready”, automatic expert context, and unconditional bias mitigation (`01-evidence.md#copy-honesty-and-destructive-actions`).
   Justification: There is no deceptive Play feature claim in the fresh listing, but two or more marketing inflations remain on the product landing page.

7. Good design is long-lasting — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: The product combines neon cyan, dark glass panels, grid/noise texture, badges, and oversized editorial serif display type (`01-evidence.md#product-and-listing`).
   Justification: Several visible trend markers date the interface rather than letting user content lead.

8. Good design is thorough down to the last detail — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Focus and reduced motion are considered, but labels, skip navigation, keyboard upload, contrast, raster assets, and automated accessibility checks remain rough (`01-evidence.md#accessibility-and-interaction-details`).
   Justification: More than three important edge details are unfinished despite good state foundations.

9. Good design is environmentally friendly — Score: 1/3
   Evidence: Initial raw JavaScript is 605.4 KiB and CSS 74.4 KiB; reduced motion exists (`01-evidence.md#reliability-performance-and-android`).
   Justification: The bundle falls in the rubric's 500 KiB–2 MiB band and ships a large monolithic surface up front.

10. Good design is as little design as possible — Score: 1/3
    Evidence: Builder exposes 41 controls, three groups of actions/destinations are duplicated, and most UI lives in a 6,200-line component (`01-evidence.md#structure-and-usefulness`).
    Justification: More than five controls or duplicated affordances can be removed or progressively disclosed without breaking the task.

Total: **12/30**
